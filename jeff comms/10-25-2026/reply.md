Thanks for all the great work on this project.

**Q1 — one event, then a ruling or a discussion, nothing changes afterwards: borderline, or not?**

Sorry for the inconsistency here. I think we should just say these cases and similar ones are not a story rather than that they are borderline.  (My waffling was due to the tricky business of how to evaluate speech or a speech-act. They are not really events but can lead to change. For example, in this last case, Shmuel orders R. Benai to sustain the daughters. You could argue that the interaction implies that R. Benai did so. But it is not really stated explicitly. So probably better to consider such cases as not even stories rather than borderline.)

**2A — Ketubot 65a, Rav Yosef bar Rava's wife before Rav Nehemya**

Borderline. In this case, it is a little more than a ruling. It is a courtroom drama, and the rabbi "apportions" wine for the woman, that is, grants her a wine allowance. I think this creates more dynamism, that is, more change. So it is borderline. Had the rabbi just stated something, ("Said R. Ami...") it would not even be borderline.

**2B — Yevamot 45a, the son of a gentile and a Jewish woman before Rav Yehuda / Rava**

Yes, I think we can reject this one. Not a story.

**2C — Gittin 89a-b, the rumour that a woman was betrothed to one of a family's sons**

This one too should not be considered a story

Thanks for being on top of these difficult cases
