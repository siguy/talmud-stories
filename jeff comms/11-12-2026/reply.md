1. Arakhin 23b, "Moshe bar Atzrei was a guarantor..." I said no. Moshebar Atzrei as guarantor of his son Rav Huna's ketubah.

Not a story. The narrative elements appear within the give and take of the legal discussion to work out the law. They are hypothetical or possible answers, not events. 

2. Pesachim 66b, "This is precisely what the sons of Beteira askedHillel..." I said no. The sons of Beteira's question to Hillel.

Not a story. Similar to the last case. This is the Talmud discussing a story that appeared previously and trying to make sense of it. But it is a legal discussion.

3. Chullin 46a, "A certain royal army came to Pumbedita..."Borderline. Rabba and Rav Yosef flee, and Rabbi Zeira meets them witha halakha.

Borderline. Agreed. There are two events the  dialogue is not really connected to the events. So a borderline case.

4. Chullin 57b, Rabbi Shimon ben Halafta's hen with the dislocatedfemur, splinted with a reed. Borderline.

Borderline. There is some change, the hen lives, but there is hardly drama or development or real change. Some would count it a minimal story: you have a protagonist, two events (the splinting, the hen living) and change. If the choice were story or not, I would say story. But if we have the option for borderline story, I think it is borderline

5. Niddah 7b, the community following Rabbi Yehoshua while RabbiEliezer was alive, and Rabbi Eliezer after his death. Borderline.

Borderline. I agree with your classification. The return of the practice could be a literary ruling, not a real event. Even if it is, there is not much drama or development.6. Shabbat 147a, "Ulla happened to come to the house of Asi barHini..." Borderline. Should it start earlier, with the Hyrcanusincident just before it?

Not a story. This is a legal ruling, a question and answer. It start with the setting, that Ulla visited Asi bar Hini. But that is not enough for a story.
