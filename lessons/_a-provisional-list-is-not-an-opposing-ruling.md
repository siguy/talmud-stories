# A provisional list is not an opposing ruling

**Date:** 2026-10-09
→ [`2026-10-09-jeff-answers-four-lines.md`](../docs/findings/2026-10-09-jeff-answers-four-lines.md)

## The rule

**When the expert's 2005 list disagrees with a rule he has stated since, apply the rule and
annotate the list entry. Do not put the disagreement to him as "two of your rulings in
conflict."** Ask only when the rule itself is unclear on the case.

## What happened

The 2026-10-02 email framed all four questions as *"two of your own rulings point in
different directions, usually a recent note against your 2005 list."* Jeff answered, and
told Simon that the 2005 lists were initial, non-comprehensive attempts, that
inconsistencies in them are expected, and that his recent thinking is what we should use.
On three of the eight passages his answer overruled his own list outright (Yevamot 107b,
Ketubot 21b, 53a). We had been treating a draft as an opposing judgment, and spending his
scarce review time adjudicating it.

The register already called the lists *"provisional"* and *"sloppy and preliminary"* in his
words. We wrote that down and then kept weighing the lists as if they were final.

## How to apply

- A list entry a settled rule rejects: annotate it in
  `results/expert_lists/superseded_by_rule.json`; it leaves the recall denominator. The list
  text is never edited (it is still evidence of what he wrote, and when).
- Recall against a 2005 list is a **check**, not the definition of a story. A miss on an entry
  he has since rejected is not a loss.
- A question for him names the rule that is unclear and the case, not the list entry it
  contradicts.
