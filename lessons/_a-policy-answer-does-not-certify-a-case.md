# A policy answer does not certify a case

*Unnumbered on a branch by convention (`lessons/README.md`) — numbered when it lands on main.*

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Found in:** Jeff's two Gittin replies, one day apart
→ [`2026-09-02-gittin-25-verdicts.md`](../docs/findings/2026-09-02-gittin-25-verdicts.md)

## The rule

**When an expert answers a question in the abstract, book the rule and nothing else. A
passage is settled only by a verdict on that passage, with the text in front of them.**
If you find yourself writing "he said X, therefore these three cases are X" — stop. That
is an inference you made, and it has to be labelled as ours, not quoted as his.

## What happened

We asked whether the Gemara's quotation of a Mishnah story counts as a Talmudic story.
He answered the policy question clearly:

> *"the second instances (Gittin 10b, 46a, 74b), where the Talmud quotes the story from
> the Mishnah, can be included."*

Three named refs, in his own words. It reads like three settled cases, and we recorded
them as three correct proposals — a defect dissolved.

The next day he reviewed the same three passages on a page that showed him the text:

| | prose, 2026-09-01 | verdict, 2026-09-02 |
|---|---|---|
| Gittin 10b | *"can be included"* | `borderline` |
| Gittin 46a | *"can be included"* | **`no`** — *"there is no story. It is filled in by the translator"* |
| Gittin 74b | *"can be included"* | **`no`** — *"the same as 46a above"* |

And on a fourth passage, from the same email:

| | prose | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Gittin 25a | *"Yes, this should be **high confidence**"* | *"This is **borderline, not high confidence**"* |

**Three of five reversed, every one of them toward the stricter reading.**

## Why it happens, and why it is not a contradiction

His policy answer was correct and still stands: it says which *corpus* a Mishnah story
belongs to. It never claimed those particular quotations contain a story — that is a
question about the text, and answering it needs the text. When he had it, two of the
three did not qualify at all.

The failure mode is ours, and it is cheap to fall into: a policy answer arrives naming
specific refs, so it *looks* like it disposed of them. It is faster than a review round
and it feels like the same currency. It is not.

## What to do instead

- Book the rule under its slug. Book the cases as **`indicated`, awaiting a verdict** —
  never as `measured`.
- When an email answer names refs, put those refs on the next review page **anyway**. It
  costs one row and it is the only thing that can catch this.
- Watch the direction: in every case here the abstract answer was the more permissive
  one. Expect an expert reasoning from a rule to be more generous than the same expert
  reading a passage, and treat a prose answer as an upper bound.
