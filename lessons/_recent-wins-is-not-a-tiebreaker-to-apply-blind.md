# "His recent ruling wins" is not a tiebreaker to apply without measuring

**Date:** 2026-10-11
→ [`2026-10-09-borderline-definition-test.md`](../docs/findings/2026-10-09-borderline-definition-test.md) (v5)

## The rule

**When an expert's own rulings on one shape disagree across time, measure what each reading
costs on everything he has labelled before writing either into a rule — and if the later
reading costs more of his labels than it saves, ask him.** "Recent wins" settles a 2005 list
against a 2026 rule; it does not settle February 2026 against April 2026.

## What happened

R-C7 ("borderline = part of a story") recovered 56 passages the judges had wrongly rejected.
Six of his `no`s came along. Their shared shape — one event, then a ruling or discussion —
he had called borderline in February and `not` from April. We narrowed R-C7 to the April
reading on "recent wins". Re-measured: it saved one `no` and returned 19 of his yes/borderline
passages to `not`, three of them passages he had himself called borderline. Reverted; the
shape went to him as a question.

## How to apply

- Before narrowing a rule to one of his rulings, count his labels on both sides of it.
- A disagreement between two of his recent rulings is a question for him, with both sides
  shown — not something to settle by date.
