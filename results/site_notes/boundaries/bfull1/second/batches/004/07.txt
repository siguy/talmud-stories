Story code: E9BE15
Story at Menachot 68b.
Change: TRIM / EXTEND

[1] - (Menachot 68b)
<b>Ravina said: My mother told me: Your father would eat from</b> the <b>new</b> crop <b>only on the evening</b> at the conclusion <b>of the seventeenth</b> of Nisan, <b>leading into the eighteenth.</b> The reason for this was that <b>he held in accordance with</b> the opinion of <b>Rabbi Yehuda</b> that nowadays it is prohibited to eat of the new crop on the sixteenth of Nisan by Torah law, <b>and</b> he was therefore <b>concerned for</b> the <b>uncertainty</b> that perhaps the sixteenth of Nisan was really the fifteenth, and consequently the seventeenth was really the sixteenth. Therefore he waited until the eve of the eighteenth, when he could be sure that there was no prohibition by Torah law against eating from the new crop.

[2] - (Menachot 68b)
<strong>MISHNA:</strong> Sacrifice of <b>the <i>omer</i></b> offering <b>would permit</b> consumption of the new crop <b>in</b> the rest of <b>the country</b> outside the Temple, <b>and the two loaves</b> offering would permit the sacrifice of the new crop <b>in the Temple. One may not bring meal offerings, or first fruits, or the meal offering</b> brought with libations accompanying <b>animal</b> offerings, from the new crop <b>prior to</b> the sacrifice of <b>the <i>omer</i>, and if he brought</b> them from the new crop they are <b>unfit.</b> After the <i>omer</i> but <b>prior to the two loaves one may not bring</b> these offerings from the new crop, but <b>if he brought</b> them from the new crop, they are <b>fit.</b>

[3] ADDED (Menachot 68b)
<strong>GEMARA:</strong> The mishna teaches that meal offerings brought from the new crop prior to the sacrifice of the <i>omer</i> offering are unfit, whereas those brought after the <i>omer</i> but prior to the two loaves are fit. <b>Rabbi Tarfon sat and posed</b> the following <b>difficulty: What is</b> the difference <b>between</b> meal offerings brought <b>before the <i>omer</i></b> and those brought <b>before the two loaves?</b>

[4] ADDED (Menachot 68b)
Rabbi <b>Yehuda bar Neḥemya said before</b> Rabbi Tarfon: <b>No,</b> one cannot compare the two situations. <b>If you said</b> that this is the <i>halakha</i> with regard to <b>before</b> the <b><i>omer</i></b> sacrifice, this is because at that stage there are <b>no</b> circumstances in which the new crop’s <b>general prohibition was permitted,</b> even <b>with regard to an ordinary</b> person; <b>shall you</b> also <b>say</b> that this is the <i>halakha</i> with regard to <b>before</b> the sacrifice of <b>the two loaves, when</b> the new crop’s <b>general prohibition was permitted with regard to an ordinary</b> person? The new crop ingredient in the meal offering is at least permitted in consumption after the <i>omer</i> is brought. Therefore, the meal offerings that were brought after the <i>omer</i> but before the two loaves are fit.

[5] KEPT (Menachot 68b)
<b>Rabbi Tarfon was silent,</b> and <b>Rabbi Yehuda ben Neḥemya’s face brightened. Rabbi Akiva said to him: Yehuda, has your face brightened because you answered the elder? I will be astonished if the days of your life will be lengthy. Rabbi Yehuda, son of Rabbi Ilai, said: That period</b> in which that interaction occurred <b>was half</b> a month <b>before Passover. When I ascended</b> again to the study hall <b>for</b> the festival of <b><i>Shavuot</i>, I asked about him: Where is</b> Rabbi <b>Yehuda ben Neḥemya? And they said to me: He passed away and left</b> this world.

[6] - (Menachot 68b)
<b>Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak says: According to the statement of</b> Rabbi <b>Yehuda ben Neḥemya, libations</b> from <b>first fruits that one brought before the <i>omer</i></b> offering is sacrificed should be <b>valid.</b> The reason is that the prohibition of the new crop does not apply to fruits at all, and therefore they are never prohibited to ordinary people. When the mishna states that first fruits are prohibited it is referring to first fruits of grain, not the fruit of a tree. The Gemara asks: Isn’t it <b>obvious</b> that libations from first fruits brought before the <i>omer</i> offering are valid? Why would one think that they should not be valid?

[7] - (Menachot 68b)
The Gemara answers that Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak’s statement is necessary, <b>lest you say: It is</b> only <b>there,</b> in the case of the meal offering of grain brought after the <i>omer</i> sacrifice, that it is valid, <b>as</b> the new crop’s <b>general prohibition was permitted with regard to an ordinary</b> person. <b>But here,</b> since with regard to these fruits there are <b>no</b> circumstances in which <b>its general prohibition was permitted,</b> one might say that the libation should <b>not</b> be valid. Therefore, Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak <b>teaches us</b> that the opposite is the case: <b>All the more so here</b> it is a valid offering, <b>where</b> the fruit <b>was not prohibited at all.</b>
