[1] - (Shabbat 151b)
The Gemara returns to verses from Ecclesiastes that relate to death and old age. It is written: <b>“Before the sun and the light</b> and the moon and the stars <b>are darkened,</b> and the clouds return after the rain” (Ecclesiastes 12:2). The Sages taught: “The sun and the light”; <b>this</b> is referring to <b>the forehead and the nose</b> which stick out from a person’s face. <b>“And the moon”; this</b> is referring to <b>the soul,</b> which shines within a person. <b>“And the stars”; these are the cheeks. “And the clouds return after the rain”; this is the light of a person’s eyes, which goes</b> and dwindles <b>after one cries</b> as if it were covered by clouds. <b>Shmuel said:</b> With regard to <b>the tear</b> that a person cries, <b>until</b> one reaches the age of <b>forty years, one’s vision returns</b> and is not harmed. <b>From here on,</b> once a person reaches the age of forty, <b>it does not return,</b> and every time a person cries his vision is weakened.

[2] - (Shabbat 151b)
<b>And</b> similarly, <b>Rav Naḥman said:</b> With regard to medicinal <b>eye shadow, until</b> one reaches the age of <b>forty years, it enhances</b> his eyesight. <b>From here and on, even if one fills it</b> as thick <b>as a weaver’s pin [<i>avisana degirda’ei</i>], it will maintain</b> his eyesight but <b>it will not enhance</b> it. The Gemara asks about this: <b>What is he teaching us</b> with the example of a weaver’s pin? The Gemara answers: Generally speaking, <b>the more eye shadow</b> one applies, <b>the more beneficial</b> it is.

[3] KEPT (Shabbat 151b)
The Gemara relates a story: When <b>Rabbi Ḥanina’s daughter died, he did not cry over her. His wife said to him: Have you</b> merely <b>let a chicken out of your house,</b> that you do not show any signs of sorrow? <b>He said to her:</b> If I cry, I will suffer <b>twice,</b> from <b>bereavement and</b> from <b>blindness.</b> The Gemara explains: <b>He held in accordance with the</b> statement that <b>Rabbi Yoḥanan said in the name of Rabbi Yosei ben Ketzarta: There are six</b> types of <b>tears, three</b> of which <b>are good</b> for the eyes <b>and three</b> of which <b>are bad.</b> Tears that come <b>from smoke, and from crying</b> out of sorrow,

[5] - (Shabbat 152a)
The Gemara continues to interpret verses from the Book of Ecclesiastes. The verse states: <b>“On the day when the keepers of the house shall tremble,</b> and the strong men <b>shall bow themselves,</b> and the grinders cease because they are few, and those that look out the windows shall be dimmed” (Ecclesiastes 12:3). <b>“On the day when the keepers of the house shall tremble”; this</b> is referring to <b>the flanks and ribs</b> that surround and protect a person’s internal organs. <b>“And the strong men shall bow themselves”; these are the thighs,</b> which support a person’s strength. <b>“And the grinders cease”; these are the teeth,</b> which decay and fall out. <b>“And those that look out the windows shall be dimmed”; these are the eyes,</b> which become dimmer.

[6] - (Shabbat 152a)
The Gemara relates: The Roman <b>emperor said to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Ḥananya: What is the reason you did not come to the House of Avidan?</b> This was a place in which dialogues and debates were conducted. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Ḥananya <b>said to him</b> enigmatically: <b>The snowy mountain is surrounded with ice,</b> meaning that his hair had turned white; <b>his dogs do not bark,</b> meaning that his voice could no longer be heard; <b>his grinders have ceased grinding,</b> meaning that his teeth had fallen out. In <b>the school of Rav they say</b> that he added: <b>I am searching for that which I have not lost,</b> because an old man walks bent over and appears to be searching for something.
