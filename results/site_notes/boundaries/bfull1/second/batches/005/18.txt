Story at Sukkah 28b.
Change: KEEP (nothing changed)

[1] - (Sukkah 28b)
<b>The Master said</b> in the <i>baraita</i>: <b>“All</b> the homeborn” comes <b>to include the minors</b> capable of performing this mitzva. The Gemara asks: <b>Didn’t we learn</b> in the mishna: <b>Women and slaves and minors are exempt from the</b> mitzva of <b><i>sukka</i>?</b> The Gemara answers: This is <b>not difficult. Here,</b> in the <i>baraita</i> where it is taught that minors are included, it is referring to <b>a minor who reached</b> the age of <b>training,</b> whose parents are commanded to train him in the performance of mitzvot and to accustom him to fulfill them. <b>Here,</b> in the mishna where it stated that the minor is exempt, it is referring to <b>a minor</b> who <b>did not</b> yet <b>reach</b> the age of <b>training.</b> The Gemara asks: The obligation of <b>a minor who reached</b> the age of <b>training</b> to perform mitzvot <b>is by rabbinic law,</b> and therefore it is not derived from a verse. The Gemara answers: Indeed, the obligation of the minor is <b>by rabbinic law</b> as part of his training, <b>and the verse</b> is <b>a mere support</b> alluding to that obligation.

[2] - (Sukkah 28b)
The mishna continues: <b>A minor who does not need his mother</b> any longer <b>is obligated</b> in the mitzva of <i>sukka</i>. The Gemara asks: <b>What are the circumstances</b> of <b>a minor who does not need his mother? In the school of Rabbi Yannai they said:</b> This is referring to <b>any</b> child <b>who defecates and his mother does not</b> need to <b>wipe him. Rabbi Shimon says:</b> It is <b>any</b> child <b>who awakens from his sleep and does not call: Mother, mother.</b> The Gemara asks: <b>Older</b> children <b>also call</b> for their mother when they arise; what, then, is the criterion? The Gemara answers: <b>Rather, say</b> that <b>any</b> child <b>who awakens and does not call: Mother, mother,</b> repeatedly until his mother comes is characterized as one who does not need his mother. An older child will cry once. However, if his mother does not come, he will tend to himself.

[3] KEPT (Sukkah 28b)
The mishna relates: There was <b>an incident where the daughter-in-law of</b> Shammai the Elder <b>gave birth</b> and he removed part of the roof so the baby would be in a <i>sukka</i>. The Gemara asks: Does the mishna cite <b>an incident to contradict</b> the preceding <i>halakha</i> that minors that are not independent are exempt from the mitzva of <i>sukka</i>? The Gemara answers: The mishna is <b>incomplete, and it teaches the following: And Shammai is stringent</b> even with very small children; <b>and there was also an incident and the daughter-in-law of Shammai the Elder gave birth</b> and Shammai <b>removed the</b> coat of <b>plaster</b> from the roof and left the beams <b>and roofed</b> with the beams <b>over the bed for the</b> newborn <b>minor.</b>

[4] - (Sukkah 28b)
<strong>MISHNA:</strong> <b>All seven days</b> of <i>Sukkot</i>, <b>a person renders his <i>sukka</i></b> his <b>permanent</b> residence <b>and his house</b> his <b>temporary</b> residence. If <b>rain fell, from when is it permitted to vacate</b> the <i>sukka</i>? It is permitted <b>from</b> the point that it is raining so hard <b>that the congealed dish will spoil.</b> The Sages <b>told a parable: To what is this matter comparable?</b> It is comparable <b>to a servant who comes to pour wine for his master, and he pours a jug [<i>kiton</i>] of water in his face</b> to show him that his presence is not desired. So too, in the <i>sukka</i>, rain is an indication that the Holy One, Blessed be He, does not want the person to fulfill the mitzva of <i>sukka</i>.

[5] - (Sukkah 28b)
<strong>GEMARA:</strong> <b>The Sages taught: All seven days</b> of <i>Sukkot</i>, <b>a person renders his <i>sukka</i></b> his <b>permanent</b> residence <b>and his house</b> his <b>temporary</b> residence. <b>How so? If he has beautiful vessels, he takes them up to the <i>sukka</i>,</b> which was typically built on the roof. <b>If he has beautiful bedding, he takes it up to the <i>sukka</i>. He eats and drinks and relaxes in the <i>sukka</i>.</b> The Gemara asks: <b>From where are these matters</b> derived? The Gemara explains that it is <b>as the Sages taught:</b> “In <i>sukkot</i> <b>shall you reside” (</b>Leviticus 23:42), and they interpreted: Reside <b>as you dwell</b> in your permanent home. <b>From here they said: All seven days, a person renders his <i>sukka</i></b> his <b>permanent</b> residence <b>and his house</b> his <b>temporary</b> residence. <b>How so? If he has beautiful vessels, he takes them up to the <i>sukka</i>;</b> if he has <b>beautiful bedding, he takes it up to the <i>sukka</i>; he eats and drinks and relaxes in the <i>sukka</i> and studies</b> Torah <b>in the <i>sukka</i>.</b>
