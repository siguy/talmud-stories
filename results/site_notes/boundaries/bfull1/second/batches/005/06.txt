Story code: CE5A06
Story at Sanhedrin 31a.
Change: TRIM / EXTEND

[1] - (Sanhedrin 31a)
The Gemara relates: There was <b>a certain child who was taken to court before Rav Naḥman. They said to him: Do you have witnesses</b> on your behalf? The child <b>said to them: No.</b> They continued to ask: <b>Do you have evidence?</b> The child <b>said to them: No. Rav Naḥman deemed him liable,</b> in accordance with the claim of the other litigant.

[2] - (Sanhedrin 31a)
The child <b>was walking and crying. These people heard him,</b> and <b>said to him: We know about the</b> monetary <b>matters of your father</b> and can testify on your behalf. When he brought them before Rav Naḥman, <b>Rav Naḥman said: In</b> a case like <b>this, even the Rabbis concede</b> that the testimony is accepted, <b>as a child does not know about the</b> monetary <b>matters of his father.</b> Clearly, when he said that he has no witnesses or proof, he said so out of ignorance and was mistaken; there is no concern about artifice.

[3] KEPT (Sanhedrin 31a)
The Gemara relates: There was <b>a certain woman from whose possession</b> a promissory <b>note emerged,</b> i.e., she was appointed to hold it. <b>She said to</b> the judge: <b>I know that this</b> promissory <b>note was repaid.</b> The creditor should not use it to collect. <b>Rav Naḥman deemed her</b> testimony <b>credible</b> and did not allow the creditor to collect the debt.

[4] KEPT (Sanhedrin 31a)
<b>Rava said to Rav Naḥman: In accordance with whose</b> opinion is your ruling? Is it <b>in accordance with</b> the opinion of <b>Rabbi</b> Yehuda HaNasi, <b>who says</b> that <b>letters,</b> i.e., the content of a promissory note, <b>are acquired by</b> merely <b>transferring</b> the document? In other words, there is no need to write a deed for the transfer of a monetary document from one individual’s ownership to another. By giving it to the recipient, he becomes the owner of the document. Therefore, since the promissory note is in this woman’s possession, she is considered its legal owner, and her claim that it was repaid is consequently accepted.

[5] KEPT (Sanhedrin 31a)
Rav Naḥman <b>said to him:</b> That is not the reason for my ruling; rather, <b>here it is different.</b> Here the woman’s claim is accepted in any event, <b>as,</b> since the promissory note was in her possession, <b>if she had wanted</b> to, <b>she</b> could have <b>burned it.</b> Therefore, she is presumably telling the truth.

[6] KEPT (Sanhedrin 31a)
<b>Some say</b> that there is another version of the story, according to which <b>Rav Naḥman did not deem her</b> testimony <b>credible. Rava said to Rav Naḥman: But if she had wanted</b> to,

[7] KEPT (Sanhedrin 31b)
<b>she</b> could have <b>burned it.</b> Why did you not accept her testimony?

[8] KEPT (Sanhedrin 31b)
Rav Naḥman answered him: <b>Since</b> the promissory note <b>was ratified in court, we do not say</b> that she is deemed credible to nullify its validity because <b>if she had wanted</b> to, <b>she</b> could have <b>burned it.</b> The validity of a ratified document is not nullified without evidence.

[9] REMOVED (Sanhedrin 31b)
<b>Rava raised an objection to</b> the ruling of <b>Rav Naḥman</b> from a <i>baraita</i>: <b>A receipt [<i>simfon</i>]</b> of repayment of a debt <b>upon which witnesses</b> are signed <b>is ratified by</b> means of <b>its signatories.</b> The witnesses testify that these are their signatures, and it is thereby ratified. If <b>there are no witnesses</b> signed <b>on it, but</b> the receipt <b>emerged from the possession of a third party</b> serving as a trustee, <b>or</b> if <b>it emerged after the signing of the documents,</b> i.e., the receipt was written on the promissory note beneath the content of the note and the witnesses’ signatures, it is <b>valid.</b> Rava states his objection: <b>Apparently,</b> the testimony of <b>a third party</b> serving as a trustee <b>is deemed credible,</b> as he can testify that the receipt is valid even if it was not signed by witnesses.

[10] REMOVED (Sanhedrin 31b)
The Gemara concludes: <b>The refutation of</b> the opinion of <b>Rav Naḥman is</b> indeed <b>a conclusive refutation.</b>

[11] - (Sanhedrin 31b)
§ <b>When Rav Dimi came</b> from Eretz Yisrael to Babylonia, he said that <b>Rabbi Yoḥanan said:</b> One <b>can always bring a proof</b> for his claim <b>and overturn</b> the previous verdict, <b>until his claims are stopped up,</b> i.e., until he has no more claims, <b>and he says: Come, so-and-so and so-and-so, and testify on my behalf,</b> in which case those witnesses are not allowed to testify.

[12] - (Sanhedrin 31b)
The Gemara asks: <b>This matter itself</b> is <b>difficult.</b> When <b>you say:</b> Until <b>his claims are stopped up, we arrive at,</b> i.e., this represents, the opinion of <b>the Rabbis,</b> who hold in the mishna that once a litigant stops presenting his claims, he cannot present additional claims later. <b>And</b> when <b>you then say:</b> Until he says <b>come, so-and-so and so-and-so, and testify on my behalf, we arrive at</b> the opinion of <b>Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel</b> that it is only in that case that the testimony is not accepted.
