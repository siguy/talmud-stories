Story code: 19B221
Story at Arakhin 10b.
Change: KEEP (nothing changed)

[1] - (Arakhin 10b)
<b>Rav and Shmuel</b> disagree as to the meaning of the phrase: Two, precious as gold. <b>One said: Each and every one</b> of these brass vessels <b>was</b> as <b>valuable as two</b> vessels made <b>from gold. And</b> the other <b>one said: The two of them</b> together <b>were</b> as <b>valuable as one</b> vessel made <b>from gold.</b> Similarly, <b>Rav Yosef teaches</b> the following explanation found in a <i>baraita</i>: <b>The two of them</b> together <b>were</b> as <b>valuable as one</b> vessel made <b>from gold.</b>

[2] - (Arakhin 10b)
The Gemara cites another explanation of the verse in Ezra. It <b>is taught</b> in a <i>baraita</i> that <b>Rabbi Natan says:</b> The cymbals and mortars <b>were pairs,</b> i.e., there were two cymbals and two mortars, <b>as it is stated</b> in the aforementioned verse <b>“two”; do not read</b> it as <b>two [<i>shenayim</i>], but as pairs [<i>sheniyyim</i>].</b>

[3] KEPT (Arakhin 10b)
§ <b>Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel teaches</b> in a <i>baraita</i>: The <b>Siloam</b> pool <b>used to spurt</b> forth <b>water through</b> an opening with a diameter <b>like</b> that of <b>an <i>issar</i></b> coin.<b>The king issued a command and they widened</b> the opening <b>so that its waters would increase, but</b> the waters actually <b>decreased. And they</b> subsequently <b>decreased</b> the size of the opening <b>again and it</b> once again <b>spurted</b> forth <b>water</b> as it had before. All this serves <b>to uphold that which is stated</b> in the verse: <b>“Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, neither let the mighty man glory in his might”</b> (Jeremiah 9:22), i.e., man should not think that he can accomplish anything he wishes.

[4] - (Arakhin 10b)
<b>And likewise Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel would say,</b> with regard to the musical instruments in the Temple: <b>There was no <i>hirdolim</i> in the Temple.</b> The Gemara asks: <b>What is a <i>hirdolim</i>? Abaye said:</b> It is a <b>hydraulic organ.</b> It was not used in the Temple <b>because its sound is pleasant but it disrupts the melody.</b>

[5] - (Arakhin 10b)
<b>Rava bar Sheila said</b> that <b>Rav Mattana said</b> that <b>Shmuel said: There was</b> an instrument called <b><i>magreifa</i> in the Temple.</b>
