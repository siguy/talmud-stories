Story at Taanit 9b.
Change: KEEP (nothing changed)

[1] - (Taanit 9b)
<b>Rav Yehuda said</b> that there is another sign of imminent rain: <b>Drizzle before rain</b> means that <b>rain is coming.</b> However, drizzle that falls <b>after rain</b> is a sign that the <b>rain is stopping.</b> Rav Yehuda provides an analogy by way of explanation: Drizzle <b>before rain</b> means that <b>rain is coming, and your mnemonic is a sieve:</b> Just as small quantities of flour drop from the sieve even before one begins to actively sift it, so too drizzle falls before rain. Conversely, drizzle <b>that</b> falls <b>after rain</b> signifies that the <b>rain is stopping, and your mnemonic is goat dung.</b> A goat’s initial droppings are large, whereas its latter droppings are small.

[2] - (Taanit 9b)
The Gemara relates that when <b>Ulla happened to</b> come <b>to Babylonia, he saw flying</b> clouds. <b>He said to</b> the local residents: <b>Put away</b> your <b>vessels, as</b> the <b>rain is coming now. Ultimately,</b> despite the presence of flying clouds, <b>rain did not fall. He said: Just as Babylonians are liars, so too, their rains are liars,</b> as flying clouds are a reliable sign of rain in Eretz Yisrael, but not in Babylonia.

[3] KEPT (Taanit 9b)
The Gemara recounts another incident that occurred when <b>Ulla happened to</b> come <b>to Babylonia.</b> He <b>saw a basket full of dates</b> on sale <b>for</b> one <b>dinar, and</b> he said: One can buy <b>a basket full of honey dates for a dinar, and</b> yet these <b>Babylonians do not occupy themselves with Torah.</b> In a place where excellent food is so inexpensive, and where there is no need to engage in hard labor for one’s material needs, the inhabitants should be able to occupy themselves with Torah. Ulla himself ate many dates, but <b>during the night they caused him pain</b> and diarrhea. <b>He</b> subsequently <b>said: A basket full of knives for a dinar, and</b> yet somehow these <b>Babylonians</b> are able to <b>occupy themselves with Torah,</b> despite the trouble these dates cause.

[4] - (Taanit 9b)
§ The Gemara discusses the source of rain. It <b>is taught</b> in a <i>baraita</i> <b>that Rabbi Eliezer says: The entire world drinks from the waters of the ocean [<i>okeyanos</i>],</b> i.e., evaporated ocean water is the source of rain. <b>As it is stated: “And there went up a mist from the earth and watered the whole face of the ground”</b> (Genesis 2:6). <b>Rabbi Yehoshua said to him: But the waters of the ocean are salty,</b> whereas rainwater is sweet. Rabbi Eliezer <b>said to</b> Rabbi Yehoshua: The waters <b>are sweetened in the clouds,</b> before they fall to the earth.

[5] - (Taanit 9b)
In contrast, <b>Rabbi Yehoshua says: The entire world drinks from the upper waters, as it is stated: “And it drinks water as the rain of heaven comes down”</b> (Deuteronomy 11:11). The <i>baraita</i> asks: <b>But</b> according to the opinion of Rabbi Yehoshua, <b>how do I uphold</b> the verse: <b>“And there went up a mist from the earth”?</b> Rabbi Yehoshua could answer that this verse <b>teaches that the clouds grow stronger, and rise to the firmament, and open their mouths like</b> a leather <b>bottle, and receive the rain waters</b> from above, <b>as it is stated:</b> “For He draws away the drops of water, <b>which distill rain from His vapor”</b> (Job 36:27).
