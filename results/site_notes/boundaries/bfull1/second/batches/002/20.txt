Story code: CD2D12
Story at Chullin 85b.
Change: TRIM / EXTEND

[1] - (Chullin 85b)
<b>Rav Pappa said to Abaye: And</b> does <b>Rabbi Shimon hold</b> that the prohibition against deriving benefit from <b>non-sacred</b> animals slaughtered <b>in the Temple courtyard is by Torah law?</b> Abaye <b>said to</b> Rav Pappa: <b>Yes</b> he does. <b>And we learned</b> in a mishna (<i>Temura</i> 33b) that <b>Rabbi Shimon says: Non-sacred</b> animals <b>that were slaughtered in the</b> Temple <b>courtyard shall be burned in the fire. And likewise, an undomesticated animal that was slaughtered in the Temple courtyard</b> shall be burned in the fire. Abaye continues: <b>Granted, if you say</b> the meat of a non-sacred domesticated animal slaughtered in the Temple courtyard is forbidden <b>by Torah law, this is</b> the reason why <b>we decree</b> that the meat of <b>an undomesticated animal</b> is prohibited as well, <b>due to</b> the Torah prohibition against deriving benefit from the meat of <b>a domesticated animal.</b>

[2] - (Chullin 85b)
<b>But if you say</b> the meat of domesticated animals is forbidden <b>by rabbinic law</b> then the mishna is difficult: <b>What is the reason</b> the meat of a non-sacred <b>domesticated animal</b> is forbidden? It is forbidden <b>lest one</b> see someone consuming this meat outside the Temple courtyard and <b>arrive</b> at the erroneous conclusion that it is permitted <b>to partake of sacrificial</b> meat <b>outside</b> the Temple courtyard. If so, the prohibition against deriving benefit from the meat of a non-sacred domesticated animal is itself a rabbinic <b>decree, and will we</b> then <b>proceed to issue a decree</b> prohibiting one to derive benefit from a non-sacred undomesticated animal in order <b>to</b> prevent the violation of <b>a decree?</b>

[3] KEPT (Chullin 85b)
§ The mishna teaches that one who stabs an undomesticated animal or bird, or who tears loose its windpipe and gullet, is exempt from covering the blood. With regard to this the Gemara relates: <b>Moths infested the flax of Rabbi Ḥiyya. He came before Rabbi</b> Yehuda HaNasi to ask how to get rid of the moths. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi <b>said to him: Take a bird and slaughter</b> it <b>over the tub of water</b> in which the flax was soaked, <b>as</b> the moths will <b>smell the blood and leave</b> the flax.

[4] REMOVED (Chullin 85b)
The Gemara asks: <b>How could</b> Rabbi Ḥiyya <b>do this? But isn’t it taught</b> in a <i>baraita</i>: <b>One who slaughters</b> a bird or an undomesticated animal, <b>and</b> does so only because he <b>needs the blood, is</b> nevertheless <b>obligated to cover</b> the blood and may not use it for any other purpose? Rather, <b>how should</b> one who needs the blood <b>act?</b> He <b>either stabs it or tears loose</b> its windpipe and its gullet, so that it is not killed with an act of slaughter. How, then, could Rabbi Ḥiyya use the blood of the bird that he slaughtered to rid his flax of the moths?

[5] REMOVED (Chullin 85b)
The Gemara responds: <b>When Rav Dimi came</b> from Eretz Yisrael, <b>he said</b> that Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi <b>said to</b> Rabbi Ḥiyya: <b>Go out</b> and <b>render</b> the bird <b>a <i>tereifa</i>,</b> and then slaughter it, as the mishna teaches that one who slaughters a <i>tereifa</i> is not required to cover its blood. <b>When Ravin came</b> from Eretz Yisrael, <b>he said</b> that Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi <b>said to</b> Rabbi Ḥiyya: <b>Go out</b> and <b>tear loose</b> the bird’s windpipe and gullet.

[6] - (Chullin 85b)
The Gemara asks: <b>According to the one who says</b> that Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi said to Rabbi Ḥiyya: <b>Go out</b> and <b>render</b> the bird <b>a <i>tereifa</i>, what is the reason</b> that Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi <b>did not say: Go out</b> and <b>tear loose</b> its windpipe and gullet?

[7] - (Chullin 85b)
<b>And if you would say</b> the reason is because Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi <b>holds</b> that <b>slaughter of a bird is not</b> obligatory <b>by Torah law, and</b> consequently <b>the tearing loose</b> of <b>its</b> windpipe and gullet <b>is</b> considered <b>its slaughter</b> such that he would be required to cover the blood, this is untenable. As <b>isn’t it taught</b> in a <i>baraita</i> that <b>Rabbi</b> Yehuda HaNasi <b>says:</b> The verse states: “And you shall slaughter…<b>as I have commanded you”</b> (Deuteronomy 12:21). This <b>teaches that Moses was</b> previously <b>commanded</b> concerning the mitzvot of slaughter, i.e., he was taught <b>about the gullet and the windpipe,</b> that the cutting of these <i>simanim</i> constitutes slaughter, <b>and about</b> the requirement to cut the <b>majority of one</b> <i>siman</i> <b>of a bird, and about</b> the requirement to cut <b>the majority of two</b> <i>simanim</i> <b>of an animal.</b> If so, Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi holds that the slaughter of a bird is obligatory by Torah law.
