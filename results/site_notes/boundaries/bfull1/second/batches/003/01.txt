Story at Chullin 86b.
Change: TRIM / EXTEND

[1] - (Chullin 86b)
<b>And Rabbi Yehuda</b> responds to this: The source <b>for separating</b> the obligations with regard to an undomesticated animal and a bird <b>is derived from</b> the verse: “And he shall pour out <b>its blood”</b> (Leviticus 17:13). The verse makes reference to the blood of only one animal, indicating that the obligation applies after slaughtering either a bird or an undomesticated animal. <b>And the Rabbis</b> respond that <b>“its blood”</b> also <b>indicates many,</b> as the term: Blood, can refer to any amount of blood. This is demonstrated by that <b>which is written: “For as to the life of all flesh, the blood thereof is all one with the life thereof”</b> (Leviticus 17:14).

[2] - (Chullin 86b)
§ <b>Rabbi Ḥanina says:</b> Although Rabbi Yehuda holds that one first covers the blood of an undomesticated animal before slaughtering the bird, <b>Rabbi Yehuda would concede with regard to the matter of</b> the <b>blessing</b> over their slaughter, i.e., <b>that one recites only one blessing.</b> The Gemara questions this assertion: <b>Ravina said to Rav Aḥa, son of Rava, and some say</b> that <b>Rav Aḥa, son of Rava,</b> said <b>to Rav Ashi:</b> In <b>what</b> way <b>is</b> this case <b>different from</b> the incident that occurred with <b>the students of Rav?</b>

[3] KEPT (Chullin 86b)
<b>As</b> it occurred that <b>Rav Beruna and Rav Ḥananel, the students of Rav, were sitting</b> together <b>at a meal,</b> and <b>Rav Yeiva the Elder stood over them</b> to serve them. <b>They said to him: Give</b> us a cup of wine over which <b>to recite the blessings</b> of Grace after Meals. <b>They then</b> changed their mind and <b>said to him: Give</b> us a cup of wine <b>to drink. Rav Yeiva the Elder said to them</b> that <b>this</b> is what <b>Rav said: Once</b> someone at a meal <b>says: Give</b> me a cup over which <b>to recite the blessings</b> of Grace after Meals, it is <b>prohibited for him to drink</b> any more <b>wine,</b> since he has expressed his desire to conclude his meal. If he now wishes to drink more wine, he must recite a blessing before drinking it. Ravina asks: <b>Here too, since he is required to cover</b> the blood of the undomesticated animal before slaughtering the bird, there is an interruption between the acts of slaughter, and <b>he has</b> therefore <b>become obligated to</b> recite a new <b>blessing</b> before slaughtering the bird.

[4] REMOVED (Chullin 87a)
The Gemara rejects this: <b>How can</b> these cases <b>be compared? There,</b> in the incident involving the students of Rav, it is <b>impossible to drink and recite a blessing simultaneously.</b> Accordingly, by requesting a cup over which to recite the blessing of Grace after Meals, they demonstrated their desire to cease drinking. <b>Here,</b> when one covers the blood of the undomesticated animal before slaughtering the bird, it is <b>possible to slaughter</b> the bird <b>with the one</b> hand <b>and cover</b> the blood of the undomesticated animal <b>with the</b> other <b>one.</b> Accordingly, the act of covering the blood of the undomesticated animal is not considered an interruption of the acts of slaughter, since they could have been performed simultaneously.

[5] - (Chullin 87a)
<strong>MISHNA:</strong> If one <b>slaughtered</b> an undomesticated animal or bird <b>and did not cover</b> the blood, <b>and another</b> person <b>saw</b> the uncovered blood, the second person is <b>obligated to cover</b> the blood. If one <b>covered</b> the blood <b>and it was</b> then <b>uncovered,</b> he is <b>exempt from covering it</b> again. If <b>the wind</b> blew earth on the blood and <b>covered it,</b> and it was consequently uncovered, he is <b>obligated to cover</b> the blood.

[6] - (Chullin 87a)
<strong>GEMARA:</strong> <b>The Sages taught</b> in a <i>baraita</i>: The verse states: <b>“And he shall pour out</b> its blood <b>and cover</b> it with earth” (Leviticus 17:13), indicating that <b>the one who poured out</b> its blood, i.e., slaughtered the animal, <b>shall cover it.</b> If one <b>slaughtered</b> the animal or bird <b>and did not cover</b> the blood, <b>and another person saw</b> the uncovered blood, <b>from where</b> is it derived <b>that</b> the person who saw the blood <b>is obligated to cover</b> it? It is derived from the following verse, <b>as it is stated: “Therefore I said to the children of Israel”</b> (Leviticus 17:12), which is <b>a warning to all the children of Israel</b> to fulfill the mitzva of covering the blood.
