Story code: 6C1104
Story at Eruvin 52a.
Change: TRIM / EXTEND

[1] - (Eruvin 52a)
The Gemara analyzes Ulla’s statement itself: <b>If he is</b> considered <b>returned,</b> with the same legal status as the rest of the residents of his city and has not established residence elsewhere, <b>why</b> is he described as <b>set out</b> on his way? <b>And if</b> he is considered <b>set out</b> on his way, indicating that he established residence at the end of his Shabbat limit, <b>why is</b> he described as <b>returned?</b>

[2] - (Eruvin 52a)
The Gemara answers: Emend Ulla’s statement and explain that <b>this is</b> what <b>he is saying: Although he was returned</b> to his original place, he is nonetheless regarded as having <b>set out</b> on his way. <b>In accordance with whose</b> opinion did he state this ruling? <b>According to</b> the opinion of <b>Rav Yosef,</b> that everyone agrees he must set out on his way, <b>and in accordance with</b> the opinion of <b>Rabbi Yosei, son of Rabbi Yehuda,</b> that he need not declare he is establishing his residence at the end of his Shabbat limit.

[3] KEPT (Eruvin 52a)
The Gemara relates that <b>Rav Yehuda bar Ishtata</b> once <b>brought a basket of fruit to Rav Natan bar Oshaya</b> in a nearby town, four thousand cubits away, on Shabbat eve. <b>When he was going,</b> Rav Natan <b>left him until he descended one step, and</b> then <b>said to him: Lodge here</b> tonight. He allowed him start his journey so that he would be considered as having set out on his way. <b>On the following day</b> Rav Yehuda bar Ishtata <b>rose early and went</b> home.

[4] REMOVED (Eruvin 52b)
The Gemara comments: <b>In accordance with whose</b> opinion did Rav Natan bar Oshaya act? Apparently, it was <b>in accordance with</b> the opinion of <b>Rav Yosef</b> that everyone agrees that he must set out on his way, <b>and in accordance with</b> the opinion of <b>Rabbi Yosei bar Yehuda</b> that he need not declare that he is establishing his residence at the end of his Shabbat limit.

[5] REMOVED (Eruvin 52b)
The Gemara rejects this suggestion: <b>No,</b> that is not necessarily so, as it is possible to say that he acted <b>according to</b> the opinion of <b>Rabba, and in accordance with</b> the opinion of <b>Rabbi Yehuda,</b> and Rav Yehuda bar Ishtata declared that he establishes his residence at the end of his Shabbat limit.

[6] - (Eruvin 52b)
We learned in the mishna that <b>Rabbi Meir says: Anyone who can establish an <i>eiruv</i>,</b> and negated his residence in his original place, and did not establish an <i>eiruv</i>, is likened to both a donkey driver and a camel driver. The Gemara asks: <b>Didn’t we have</b> already <b>learned</b> it <b>once</b> before in another mishna: In a case of <b>uncertainty, Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda say: This</b> person <b>is</b> likened to both <b>a donkey driver</b> and <b>a camel driver.</b> Here too, it is obvious that the same applies, as that is Rabbi Meir’s opinion with regard to all uncertain cases.

[7] - (Eruvin 52b)
<b>Rav Sheshet said:</b> It is necessary to state this ruling here as well, so that <b>you will not say the reason</b> for <b>Rabbi Meir’s</b> statement only applies in a case where there is <b>uncertainty whether</b> one <b>established an <i>eiruv</i></b> or <b>did not establish an <i>eiruv</i>,</b> and in <b>that</b> case <b>he is</b> in likened to both <b>a donkey driver</b> and <b>a camel driver. However,</b> in a case where there is <b>certainty</b> that he <b>did not establish an <i>eiruv</i> he is not</b> likened to both <b>a donkey driver</b> and <b>a camel driver,</b> but his Shabbat limit is the same as the rest of the residents of his city.
