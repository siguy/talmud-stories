**Subject:** One review page — 10 passages, and a finding about twins

Jeff,

Where things stand: we can now find about 94 of every 100 stories on your Yevamot list
without having seen it, and the biggest remaining gap turned out to have one cause. This
email is one short review page and the story behind it.

**The finding.** When we went through every story of yours we miss, 18 of 38 were sitting
one line away from a story we *had* found — and in every one of those 18, the story we
found was the missed one's twin. The Talmud tells the same shape of anecdote two or three
times in a row with different actors: R. Zadok and the noblewoman, then Rav Kahana and
the noblewoman; *"who is of the house of Ḥasa? Ḥasa has drowned,"* then the same cry for
Ḥiwai. We were returning one and treating the next as its continuation.

The fix that worked was not telling the machine to look harder. It was asking it a
narrower question: *here is the story we found; here is the passage beside it; is that a
separate incident, the same story, or not a story?* On Yevamot that recovered all six of
your twins we had missed — both 121b criers, 63a, 64b, 34b. It also proposed eight
passages your 2005 list does not carry; I read them first and threw out the four obvious
ones. The other four are on the page.

**The page — 10 passages, four questions, attached.** Each asks first whether it is a
story; extent and confidence sit behind a disclosure and you can ignore them.

1. **Kiddushin 39b** (1) — a passage our matching had wrongly credited to your list. I
   think the story is actually the segment just before the one we marked — is it a story,
   and where does it start?
2. **Gittin 57b, 68a** (2) — Nebuzaradan and the blood of Zechariah; Solomon sends
   Benayahu for Ashmedai. Nobody has judged these. I'm fairly sure they are yours, and
   that 68a begins earlier than we marked.
3. **Ketubot 7a, 15a, 112a** (3) — all speech, nothing else happens. You said
   "borderline" is the right column for these rather than a general rule. Is it, for
   these three? (112a is the interesting one — is being mocked an event?)
4. **Yevamot 15a, 17a, 105a, 106b** (4) — what the twin pass proposed that your list does
   not carry. Your verdicts here decide whether that pass becomes the default.

One thing we learned the hard way, which you may find familiar: for two days our results
looked as if the model had gotten worse overnight, and it took that long to notice that a
one-line edit had cut the examples out of the prompt. Nothing failed; the answers were
just worse. The lesson we wrote down was *diff the prompt before blaming the model.*

Thank you, as always.
