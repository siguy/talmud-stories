Dear Jeff,

Your 2005 Ketubot list turned out to be the most useful thing we have. Used as a blind test — it predates the detector by twenty years — we find 143 of your 149 stories, 96%. That is the first honest recall number this project has had. Your remark that the AI found stories your own lists missed has cut both ways: your list is now the only reason we know what we are missing.

One question, and one ask.

The question. Because you wrote each story out in full, we could recover your 2005 boundaries and compare them with the corrections you have sent this year. They line up on where a story begins and part ways on where it ends — the 2005 list keeps the legal material around a story, the recent notes say to cut it. Not a contradiction; the two were made for different purposes. But it leaves us with something neither answers.

When a ruling is what makes a passage a story at all — your example, “A man stole another man’s cow and sold it. Rava ruled…” — is that ruling part of the story we display, or the legal discussion that follows it? It matters more than it sounds: in about a fifth of the entries we currently trim, the ruling is precisely the thing that showed the event was real rather than hypothetical, and cutting it leaves a reader unable to tell the two apart.

Two small things, not questions. On “jumped up and stated” you told us to establish criteria and hold to them, so we have: speech alone is not enough, an emotional reaction counts as an event, and the rest are marked borderline for database users.
