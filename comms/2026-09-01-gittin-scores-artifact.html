Four things that can go wrong, measured separately

A story can be lost at four different points, and for a long time we could only see the total. Splitting them tells us which one to fix — and three of the four have quite different costs. Text flows left to right; a loss early cannot be recovered later.

Where our boundary differs from yours

On 171 boundaries we match exactly 84% of the time. The differences are lopsided in a way that is worth seeing. At the end, we usually stop earlier than you — the legal give-and-take you said need not be quoted. At the start, we are almost always one clause late, and it is the same clause every time: the formula that introduces the story.

Gittin 58a · segment 10 — you start at the attribution, we start at the narrative

the clause you include and we drop where we both agree the story runs

The same clause, across three tractates: תַּנְיָא · תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן · תָּא שְׁמַע · כִּי אֲתָא רַב דִּימִי אָמַר · רַב חָנִין מִישְׁתַּעֵי. It accounts for 27 of the 30 late starts we have.

We tried to settle it with a rule — always reach back over the formula. Measured across all 470 boundaries we can check, it fixed nine and broke eight. You start after the formula about as often as before it, so no mechanical rule can tell the cases apart. That is why it is a question rather than a fix.
