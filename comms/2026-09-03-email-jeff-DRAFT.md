Dear Jeff,

Your speech-act point is right, and testing it turned up something I did not expect —
about our text spans rather than about the criteria.

**First, the word list works.** I built it and ran it, and the immediate effect was to
catch a mistake we had made that same morning. We had screened the whole low-confidence
bucket for "does anything non-speech happen," and it cleared a batch of passages on the
grounds that something did — where the something was *"retracted, came, stood"*,
*"sent the question"*, and *"brought the case."* Your four words, doing exactly what you
said they do. Without the list we would have counted them as events.

**One adjustment I'd suggest.** Two of your four flip depending on their object, so the
list can flag a passage for scrutiny but shouldn't decide it on its own:

- *sent* — a **message or question** is speech; a **messenger, a gift, a document** is an event
- *retracted* — an **opinion** is speech; a written **get** is an event

I'd add *came before*, *brought before*, and *sat* to the scrutiny list. "Came before"
was by a distance the most common action-shaped non-action in the set — it appears in
31 of the 110 passages I screened.

**Now the unexpected part, which I think matters more.** When a passage came out as pure
speech, I went back and looked at the text just after where our span ends. In most cases
the action was sitting right there, one or two lines outside our boundary:

- **Ketubot 111a** — our span ends with a man asking whether he should travel to Babylonia. The next line is *"and he died."*
- **Ketubot 10a** — ends on the exchange; next comes *"they brought him the cloth, and he soaked it in water and laundered it."*
- **Ketubot 66b** — the next lines have her *riding on a donkey, leaving Jerusalem, gathering barley.*
- **Ketubot 65a** — the next line is *"I should not have to be ashamed"* — an emotional reaction, which by your July rule counts.

So a good part of what looked like "speech-acts masquerading as stories" is our own
boundaries stopping too early. **Our error, not the tradition's**, and a different fix
from the one your email proposes. I would rather tell you that than send you a tidy
count.

**What I think survives as a real criteria question — three passages:**

- **Ketubot 7a** — Rav Ami permits, the Sages object, he answers. All speech.
- **Ketubot 112a** — the Amorite and the man of Eretz Yisrael arguing about the yield of a palm tree, ending in mockery. All speech, unless the mockery itself is the event.
- **Ketubot 17a** — R. Yehuda *would* suspend Torah study for a funeral. A standing practice rather than something that happened once.

**The question, then:** for passages like these three — nothing happens but speech —
should they become **not-a-story**, stay **low confidence** as they are now, or take a
new **borderline** status the database shows the reader rather than deciding for them?
Ketubot 112a is the interesting one either way: is being mocked an event?

**And one smaller question.** You list *considered* as a speech-act word, and you've also
told me an emotional reaction — "Rabbi X was embarrassed" — counts as an event. Both are
internal. I've drawn the line at *deliberating about the law* = speech, *feeling
something* = event. Is that where you'd put it? A number of passages turn on it.

Best,
Simon
